What's happening

Every leaf begins with a random value (ember → ice spectrum = high diversity). At coarse-graining level k, leaves in the same depth-(d−k) ball are replaced by their ball's mean. Because balls are nested (strong triangle inequality), the replacement is a conditional expectation — and conditional expectation can only reduce variance.

The curve on the left is monotone non-increasing by construction. The geometry guarantees it. Uniformity is not imposed; it is inevitable.

The law of total variance

In an ultrametric space, every pair of balls is either disjoint or nested. Coarse-graining replaces a ball by its mean, which is the conditional expectation of the leaves given the ball. The law of total variance says:

Var(X) = E[Var(X | B)] + Var(E[X | B]) ≥ Var(E[X | B])

So each upward step — from leaves to parent balls — can only shrink (or hold) the diversity you observe. Diversity collapse is a theorem, not a choice.

Why this matters for hierarchy

The same mechanism that makes hierarchical clustering stable makes ultrametric coarse-graining deterministic: once you fix the tree, the convergence path is fully fixed. No hidden hand is needed — the geometry supplies the force.
